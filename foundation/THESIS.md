# Working thesis — Future Farm

## The object of inquiry

Life repeatedly produces integrated systems in which differentiated components can achieve capabilities not available to them independently. This includes metabolic cooperation, symbiosis, multicellularity, specialized tissues, social learning, cumulative human culture, and the industrial and informational systems enabling artificial intelligence. These examples do **not** demonstrate that evolution has a fixed destination or that every increase in complexity is beneficial.

Future Farm investigates whether the **underlying order of interactions** contains discoverable principles that help predict when new arrangements can expand the continued capacity of life to nourish, reproduce, cooperate, differentiate, and create. The unit of concern is all living and sentient organisms, not humanity alone.

## Order, contingency, and consequence

A collision in space may be unexpected, yet its consequences are constrained by the same physical laws as any other collision. Similarly, mutations and ecological encounters may be contingent while their outcomes remain shaped by physical, chemical, biological, and environmental constraints. The working question is whether understanding those constraints can reveal **ranges of likely future outcomes and feasible interventions**. It is not a claim that biology is as predictively tractable as celestial mechanics.

The natural laws do not change when conditions change; the consequences can.

## Capital-C Consequence (C∞)

Study the propagation of interactions through time, including immediate beneficiaries, displaced costs, feedback, ecological producers, future generations, and foregone possibilities. A locally successful consumer may damage its own future resource base. An apparent benefit to decomposers may occur only after a less destructive sequence of uses was missed.

**Calculate backward from a candidate constructive outcome**: identify necessary conditions, alternative resource pathways, signals, feedback, and constraints; then test whether the outcome is feasible and stable. This resembles backward reachability in control theory, but a general biological formula has **not** been derived.

## Constructive cascades

Seek arrangements of **place, timing, sequence, and concentration** that enable different organisms to meet needs without unnecessary destruction of the productive systems they depend on. A producer such as an apple tree need not be protected by exterminating every consumer; the objective is compatible continued nourishment across the system.

The relevant distinction is **need versus familiar means of meeting the need**. A predator needs appropriate nutrition, not necessarily the death of a particular prey animal. Whether a proposed alternative improves the whole ecological system is a separate empirical question.

## Coordination and the temporary referee

A transition toward a more mutually sustaining arrangement may require temporary help while organisms still respond to incentives of the old arrangement. The proposed referee is a **transitional coordinating function**, not a permanent sovereign or a license to intervene in ecosystems. The desired mature arrangement would be self-regulating, decentralized, responsive to feedback, and compatible with willing participation where agency is relevant.

## North Star and epistemic caution

Proposed North Star: *To understand and participate in the natural order so that life can continually sustain itself, flourish, and increase its capacity to create beyond what has existed before.*

This is a **normative and philosophical proposal**. Natural selection alone does not justify it. Scientific work must test mechanisms, quantify tradeoffs, and allow evidence to overturn favored ideas. Theological vocabulary (Logos, Comprehensive Logic, Universal Code) is defined in the companion foundation and must not be presented as established physics.
