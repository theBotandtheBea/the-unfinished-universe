# Future Farm — Open Research in Constructive Consequence

**Public working research | 17 November 2026 | Open questions, not established conclusions**

Future Farm asks whether the conditions governing life's existing relationships can be understood well enough to discover more constructive arrangements: ones that meet the needs of **all living and sentient organisms**, sustain the systems producing nourishment, and preserve or expand life's future creative capacities.

This is not a plan to optimize nature for humanity. Humanity and AI are participants and research tools, not the sole beneficiaries or appointed rulers.

## The working idea

A lion requires nutrition, not necessarily a chase. Fruit-feeding insects require resources and reproductive opportunities, not necessarily the destruction of the tree's entire crop. Fungi and bacteria need substrates, but their local proliferation should not undermine the system that supports them. **Change the location, timing, concentration, and sequence of resource access; investigate the consequences across organisms and generations.**

The claim is **not** that any suggested intervention is automatically safe or that every conflict has a solution. The research question is whether better feasible arrangements exist, and how we could know.

## Open process, not just open results

Mistakes, objections, changed minds, negative findings, and abandoned proposals belong in the record. Preserve the original statement; append an attributed correction. Do not retroactively make the research look inevitable.

## Evidence labels

**Established** — well-supported findings with traceable sources. **Research hypothesis** — testable but unverified proposal. **Analogy** — explanatory comparison, not proof. **Philosophical/theological interpretation** — not a demonstrated scientific mechanism. **Illustrative number** — example, not measured data. **Open** — unresolved.
